About me

I'm a young Tanzanian who wants to know how technology works underneath, not just which command to type.

Most of my learning happens on a smartphone. Acode and Pydroid are my editors, and a lot of my curiosity gets tested in small, honest experiments. That setup has taught me something useful: you don't need a perfect machine to start understanding systems, you need persistence and good questions.

I'm on an IT-related certificate-level path and aiming to progress toward a Diploma in Cyber Security and Digital Forensics. Alongside that, I teach myself Python, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Linux, networking, authentication and cryptography, and I try to connect each topic to the reasoning behind it.

Security draws me in because it sits where everything meets: operating systems, networks, code, human behaviour and evidence. I'm interested in the defender's side (logs, traffic, detection, SOC work), the ethical tester's side (finding weaknesses in authorized environments so they get fixed), and the investigator's side (what digital traces can prove).

What makes this journey distinctive is that I'm documenting it while it happens. This site shows the process of becoming capable, including the unfinished parts.

Understand first, memorize later. A tool is only useful once I can explain what it does and why it works.

My approach to cybersecurity

I want to understand security from both sides, because each makes the other better.

How defenders work

Detecting, investigating and responding to threats: SOC analysis, logs, network traffic and threat detection.

How ethical testers work

Finding weaknesses in authorized environments so they can be fixed: reconnaissance, vulnerability research and web security.

How investigators work

Preserving evidence and reading digital traces carefully, which is the core of digital forensics.

My rules are simple: only test what I own or am explicitly authorized to test, respect privacy, report issues responsibly, and never treat other people's systems as a playground.
